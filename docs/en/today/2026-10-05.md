# 2026-10-05

## LlamaIndex proposes three principles for Agentic OCR: adaptive strategy, evidence-based correction, bounded execution

- **Summary**: LlamaIndex publishes "OCR is Dead. Long Live Agentic OCR," defining Agentic OCR as using an LLM tool-calling loop to adaptively process a document (rather than converting it in one pass), and lays out three design principles — adaptive strategy (allocate extra processing to complex elements like tables and charts, rather than treating every page element the same), evidence-based correction (a "correction" must be backed by evidence that it actually improved the result, not just another model call producing a different answer), and bounded execution (clear objectives and hard time/cost limits that deliver a usable result, rejecting open-ended refinement loops); it argues that as agents take on more consequential tasks, document infrastructure must rise from "machine-readable" to "faithful reconstruction" — preserving layout relationships, headers, footers, and other contextual elements.
- **Why it matters**: Continuing LlamaIndex's string of document-parsing methodology posts (08-05's "OCR won't be commoditized by frontier models," 08-12's "LLM OCR errors are harder to catch"), this is the first time "Agentic OCR" is systematized into three actionable design principles rather than just described as a phenomenon, giving a concrete yardstick for judging whether something actually qualifies as Agentic OCR.
