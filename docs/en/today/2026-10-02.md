## Anthropic invests $100M in Claude Frontier Academy, targeting 10,000 trained deployment engineers

- **Summary**: Anthropic announces Claude Frontier Academy, a $100M commitment to train 10,000 "Frontier Deployed Engineers" (FDEs) by end of 2027, with the first cohorts drawn from Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley, and Novo Nordisk; the program runs a 12-week residency model — starting with a simulated enterprise deployment and graded assessment, then a medical-residency-style practicum on real casework before certification, with the first engineers expected certified in early 2027.
- **Why it matters**: Within the "delivery model spectrum" for enterprise agents, the platform-plus-embedded-engineer (FDE) model has long been constrained by the limited supply of qualified engineers — Anthropic directly funding its own talent pipeline treats "insufficient delivery capacity" as a strategic bottleneck to solve head-on, rather than passively waiting for consulting firms to train people themselves; the concrete figures ($100M, 10,000 people, 12 weeks) give a comparable scale of investment.

## ServiceNow launches AutoSynthData: generating enterprise-agent training data from capability gaps

- **Summary**: ServiceNow CoreAI launches AutoSynthData — it compares a target model's failure patterns against a stronger teacher model's successes to decide what the model should learn next, then automatically generates and validates new tasks that vary entities, initial environment state, workflow composition, tool combinations, wording, and difficulty, rather than relying on hand-curated datasets; in roughly 18 hours it generated 2,000 synthetic training samples, and supervised fine-tuning on them lifted Gemma's mean Pass@1 by 7.2 percentage points on a hybrid benchmark and from 18.77% to 27.18% on ITSM.
- **Why it matters**: This lands the synthetic-data-generation approach in the specific setting of "enterprise workflow agents" — not generic synthetic data, but data generated by working backward from a model's actual capability gaps, giving smaller teams a reproducible path to building their own specialized agent models cheaply.
